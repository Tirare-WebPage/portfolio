02 / HOW I THINK

Every architecture
solves a different problem.

These four CAD stages document a change in the engineering question—from power delivery to the conditions that make a retrofit useful.

01

Observe

Start with the vehicle, the work it already does, and the constraints that cannot be ignored.

02

Set constraints

Write down what must stay: the existing vehicle, manual rideability, serviceability and realistic installation.

03

Model

Use CAD to expose the power path, space conflicts, mounting assumptions and interactions.

04

Reject or revise

When an architecture fails an important constraint, change it. Time already spent is not evidence that a design is right.

05

Define the test

Separate what a drawing suggests, what a model calculates, what a prototype demonstrates and what a field trial would prove.

01TIRARE / ORIGINA broken pedal changed the question+

I noticed a cargo tricycle still carrying goods even though its pedal was broken. The detail that stayed with me was that the vehicle was not working as intended, but the work continued. Repair costs money and time; replacement costs more. For someone who depends on the vehicle for income, stopping is not an abstract engineering inconvenience.

That changed my question. I stopped thinking only about how to build an electric vehicle and started asking whether the vehicle people already own could be made less physically demanding to use.

This was an observation that shaped the project, not a formal survey or a claim about every operator.

02THE MACHINE THAT CARRIED THE WORKThe HP 240 G8+

Much of Tirare’s early CAD, architecture and documentation was done on an HP 240 G8 that I repaired myself after I was told it could not be fixed. It was far from a pristine workstation: the keyboard became unreliable, several keys were missing, and I continued with an external keyboard.

I had to arrange my own transport to the IIIT Delhi E-Pitch Café pitch. The laptop battery ran out in the middle of the presentation, so I kept explaining the work. The project was later selected as the Proof-of-Concept track winner and received ₹1 lakh in prototype support.

I don’t treat the laptop as a hardship prop. It’s part of the project’s record: the machine on which early versions were drawn, written, reconsidered and rebuilt.

03ENGINEERING / REVISIONWhen 96 pages were not enough+

An early Tirare approach grew into a 96-page engineering proof-of-concept. The document contained a lot of work, but that did not make the architecture sound. I found a fundamental flaw in how the drivetrain interacted with the existing axle and pedal path.

I chose to stop defending that design and rebuild the approach around the actual mechanical constraints. It meant giving up work that had taken months, but keeping a flawed design because I had invested time in it would have been worse.

Effort is not validation. A drawing is not a test. The next version has to answer the specific question the previous one left unresolved.

04F1 IN SCHOOLS / A SECOND ROUTEFinding another way to Nationals+

My first F1 in Schools team attempt fell through when the team did not show up. I contacted Shiv Nadar School directly and asked whether another opportunity was possible. I was able to join the group that became Team Seraphis and eventually served as team leader and engineer.

The car model took around six months of design and iteration under a detailed rulebook and tight constraints. The team qualified for the National Finals. The result was shared, and the work involved learning to coordinate a team as well as keep the design moving under deadlines.

I wrote about a pigeon that came to sit on my balcony and one day was visibly unwell. I was frightened of handling it and had a major Physics exam the next day. I called for help, eventually found an animal hospital willing to advise me, and spent hours trying to get the bird safely into a bag for the trip.

04 / MRII

Mechanism
before claim.

MECHANICAL RIDER INTENT INTERFACE

A rider-input signal
with a defined load path.

MRII investigates a mechanical interface for estimating human-applied effort in a human-plus-powered drivetrain. The design combines a compliant torsional coupling, whose relative angular displacement is sensed, with a downstream overrunning element intended to limit powered torque from influencing the rider-input measurement.

The physical MRII subsystem has been built and demonstrated. The complete cargo-tricycle retrofit has not yet been fully integrated and field-validated. The difference matters—and it belongs on the same page as the mechanism.

Human torque+Motor torque=Axle torque
01 / MECHANICAL INTERFACE

Compliance gives the signal a physical meaning.

Human torque deforms the compliant member. Relative angular displacement between input and output members is the torque-related measurand.

02 / ANGLE SENSING

Measure the relative rotation.

The documented prototype configuration uses an AS5047P magnetic angle sensor. The angle reading is not, by itself, proof of a calibrated torque output.

03 / TORQUE ISOLATION

Keep powered torque from corrupting rider input.

A downstream overrunning element is intended to manage reverse-side torque influence. Its effectiveness needs matched-condition testing with clutch state verified.

SUBMITTED PROJECT / ETSD0071

Quantitative Assessment of a Compliant Rider-Input Interface for Torque-Assisted Mobility

SUBMITTED FOR EVALUATION

Question

Can a compliant rider-input interface provide a useful torque-related angular signal while limiting actuator-induced apparent input in a human-plus-powered drivetrain?

Work

The paper and synopsis define the interface measurand and torque reference planes, analyse torque–angle records, examine matched-condition actuator-isolation data, compare flexure candidates, and identify the experiments needed for physical confirmation.

Current boundary

The numerical values in the current analysis must be distinguished from independently verified physical measurements. Traceable calibration and synchronized tests on a frozen assembly remain validation tasks.

ANURAG MISHRA / AMBIENCE PUBLIC SCHOOL

I don’t expect the first
answer to be right.

My work crosses mechanical design, mobility, research, robotics and writing. I have learned a lot by trying to build before I feel fully ready—and by finding out that a design, a plan or an assumption does not survive contact with reality.

I’m interested in engineering that starts with people’s actual conditions, research that says exactly what its evidence supports, and projects where changing your mind is part of doing the work well.

This portfolio is a living record. It includes completed milestones, unfinished work and questions that still need answers. I’ll update it as the evidence changes.
